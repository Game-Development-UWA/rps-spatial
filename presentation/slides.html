For a known, differentiable function, we can find the slope of the function at any point using calculus. This can be used to find important landmarks

Landmark —

Gradient Ascent

If you can find the slope at any point in the function, you can climb without seeing the whole mountain.

Take a step uphill

Move a little in the direction of the derivative. \(\alpha\) is how bold that step is: too small and you crawl, too large and you overshoot.

\[x \leftarrow x + \alpha\, f'(x)\]

Hold your horses

Newton’s method refuses a fixed \(\alpha\). It reads convexity and jumps to where a local quadratic says the slope should hit zero.

\[x \leftarrow x - \frac{f'(x)}{f''(x)}\]

A ridge, not always the summit

Ascent stops when the slope is flat. Calculus already told you that landmark might be a local maximum.

Many knobs at once

In higher dimensions the slope is the gradient: a vector of partial derivatives.

\[\theta \leftarrow \theta + \alpha\, \nabla J(\theta)\]

Single-state hill climb

No derivative, no map of the mountain. Keep one candidate. Tweak it. Move only if the tweak is better.

\[x \leftarrow x' \quad\text{if}\quad f(x') > f(x)\]

One sample vs many

Same single state; different patience with the neighbourhood.

First-choice

Sample one neighbour. If it is uphill, take it. Cheap, impatient, and often enough when most nearby tweaks still go up.

Steepest ascent

Sample several neighbours, then take the best of the batch. You spend more evaluations to pick a stronger step.

\[x \leftarrow \arg\max_{x' \in N} f(x')\]

How you tweak

A uniform draw treats every offset in a box as equally likely. A Gaussian prefers small nudges and only rarely throws a long jump.

Explore vs exploit

Larger step sizes wander farther: more exploration, less local care. Larger samples, especially in steepest ascent, squeeze more juice from the same neighbourhood: more exploitation.

I sacrificed everything for you to be here

A greedy climb stops at the first peak. Accepting a worse neighbour is a way to leave a ridge you would otherwise call home.

Simulated annealing

Take every improvement. Take a worse move with a probability that cools over time, so early wandering gives way to a late, stubborn climb.

\[P(\text{accept}) = e^{\Delta f / T}\]

Iterated local search

Climb until you stall. Kick the state far enough to leave the basin, then climb again. The search is still single-state; the kick is the memory of being stuck.
